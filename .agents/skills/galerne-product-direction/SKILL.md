# Galerne Product Direction

Galerne is a persistent terminal adventure that fits naturally around developer wait time. Manual play must stand on its own; automatic activation is an entry point into the same lasting game.

## Design for

- Give each session a clear near-term reason to act and connect it to destinations, discoveries, tools, people, and regional goals.
- Treat the current forward runner as a traversal foundation. The long-term game is exploration through real places, reconnection of the old Lines, refuge development, companions, and visible changes to the world.
- Make restored infrastructure alter routes, transport, lighting, refuge facilities, or where people live. Progress should change the world, not only add a score.
- Let the refuge grow from repurposed infrastructure. Give companions a specific role and concise, natural dialogue.
- Use developer-task duration only for a small, capped expedition benefit. Never reward deliberately slow work or penalize an optimized project; manual play uses a neutral baseline.
- Keep actions understandable across English, French, Spanish, and Simplified Chinese. Prefer readable action and environmental cues over required prose.

## Keep the experience respectful

Build engagement through mastery, curiosity, attachment, collection, and visible restoration. Do not use finite lives, long punitive restarts, daily streaks, FOMO, fake scarcity, loot boxes, or pressure to keep playing. Checkpoints should make failure quick to recover from and keep meaningful progress.

Treat current lore, region labels, item names, and character names as proposals. Choose specific language that belongs to Galerne; avoid generic fantasy, cyberpunk, grimdark, and placeholder-sounding terminology.

Automated tests can establish mechanics and state transitions. They cannot establish fun, attachment, or product identity. Gameplay direction needs human playtesting before it is accepted.
